### STEP 00-5 — LINGUISTIC MATRIX & SEMANTIC ANALYSIS (MANDATORY H6)

1. **Etymological & Root Semantics (*Ishtiqaq* — `r-q-b`, `r-q-q`, `f-k-k`):**
   - **Source:** İbn Fâris (*Mu'cemü Makâyîsi'l-Lüğa*, Vol. 2, pp. 412–414).[^1]
   - `[CITATION / NAKİL]`: «الرَّقَبَةُ: الْعُنُقُ، وَالرِّقُّ: الْعُبُودِيَّةُ، وَأَصْلُهُ الضَّعْفُ وَالدِّقَّةُ» **[TRANSLATION]** (*"Al-Raqabah denotes the neck, while al-riqq denotes servitude/bondage, whose root meaning is weakness and fragility [al-da'f wa'l-diqqah]"* — [CS-01, Vol. 2, pp. 412-414](https://shamela.ws/book/21710/972)).
   - `[ANALYSIS / YORUM]`: Etymologically, `riqq` (servitude) reflects an accidental state of vulnerability rather than an ontological human status, forming the linguistic basis for its abolition.

2. **Quranic Semantic Conceptualization (`Fakku Raqabah` — Râgıb el-İsfahânî):**
   - **Source:** Râgıb el-İsfahânî (*el-Müfredât fî Garîbi'l-Kur'ân*, Vol. 1, pp. 363–366).[^2]
   - `[CITATION / NAKİL]`: «الرَّقَبَةُ تُسْتَعْمَلُ فِي الْعَبْدِ، وَفَكُّ الرَّقَبَةِ: عِتْقُهُ وَتَخْلِيصُهُ مِنَ الرِّقِّ» **[TRANSLATION]** (*"Al-Raqabah is used by synecdoche for a captive/slave, and fakku'l-raqabah means emancipating and liberating him from the yoke of bondage"* — [CS-02, Vol. 1, pp. 363-366](https://shamela.ws/book/23636/345)).
   - `[ANALYSIS / YORUM]`: In Qur'anic usage, `fakku raqabah` ("untying a neck") frames emancipation not merely as a technical legal transaction, but as an ontological rescue of human dignity from the knot of servitude.

3. **Syntactic, Rhetorical & Broad Lexicographical Framework (İbn Manzûr, Zemahşerî & Taberî):**
   - **Sources:** İbn Manzûr (*Lisânü'l-Arab*, Vol. 1, pp. 425–427)[^3], Zemahşerî (*el-Keşşâf*, Vol. 4, pp. 314–316)[^6], and Taberî (*Câmi'u'l-Beyân*, Vol. 22, pp. 155–160).[^4]
   - `[CITATION / NAKİL]`: İbn Manzûr: «الرِّقُّ: الْعُبُودِيَّةُ، وَالْفَكُّ: التَّخْلِيصُ وَالتَّفْرِيجُ، وَفَكُّ الرَّقَبَةِ: إِعْتَاقُهَا» **[TRANSLATION]** (*"Al-riqq is servitude/bondage, al-fakk is releasing and opening, and fakku'l-raqabah is emancipating a neck"* — [CS-03, Vol. 1, pp. 425-427](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/425)); Zemahşerî: «فَلَا اقْتَحَمَ الْعَقَبَةَ فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ أَيْ تَخْلِيصُهَا مِنَ الرِّقِّ وَالْإِطْلَاقُ لِلتَّعْمِيمِ» **[TRANSLATION]** (*"'Fakku raqabah' means liberating a neck from bondage, and the unrestricted indefinite form is for universal generality [li't-ta'mîm]"* — [CS-06, Vol. 4, pp. 314-316](https://shamela.ws/book/23627/2379)). Rhetorically, `fakku raqabatin` appears in the indefinite form (*nakirah*) to establish universal generality (`li't-ta'mîm`).

- **Word-by-Word Lexical & Morphological Breakdown (*Al-Tahlil al-Lafzi*):**
  - `فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ` (fakku raqabatin): Untying a neck from the bond of servitude; emancipating a human being and restoring primordial liberty (Al-Balad 90:13).
  - `فَضَرْبَ الرِّقَابِ` (fa-darba'r-riqâbi): Breaking the military resistance of hostile combatants during active warfare; a battlefield engagement rule, not peacetime enslavement (Muhammad 47:4).
  - `فَشُدُّوا الْوَثَاقَ` (fa-shuddû'l-wathâqa): Secure the bonds of prisoners of war once armed hostility is neutralized (Muhammad 47:4).
  - `فَإِمَّا مَنًّا بَعْدُ وَإِمَّا فِدَاءً` (fa-immâ mannan ba'du wa immâ fidâ'an): Thereafter release war captives either by unconditional grace (`mann`) or by ransom/prisoner exchange (`fidâ'`) (Muhammad 47:4).
  - `مَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُمْ` (mâ malakat aymânukum): Those held under the legal trust and covenant of your right hands; a Qur'anic designation transforming captives/servants from chattel property into legal persons (An-Nur 24:33; An-Nisa 4:25, 36).
  - `فَكَاتِبُوهُمْ` (fa-kâtibûhum): Execute a written contractual emancipation agreement (`mukâtabah`) with bondspeople who request freedom (An-Nur 24:33).
  - `فَتَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ مُّؤْمِنَةٍ` (fa-tahrîru raqabatin mu'minatin): Mandatory legal expiation (`kaffârah`) requiring the emancipation of a believing bondsperson (An-Nisa 4:92).
  - `وَفِي الرِّقَابِ` (wa fi'r-riqâbi): Mandatory public budgetary allocation from Zakat revenues dedicated to liberating captives and paying off `mukâtabah` contracts (At-Tawbah 9:60).

- **Classical Exegetical Synthesis (Taberî, Cessâs & İbn Âşûr):**
  - `[CITATION]` `[NAKİL]`: Taberî (*Câmi'u'l-Beyân*, Vol. 22, pp. 155–160): «فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ: عِتْقُهَا وَتَخْلِيصُهَا مِنَ الرِّقِّ، وَإِمَّا مَنًّا بَعْدُ وَإِمَّا فِدَاءً» **[TRANSLATION]** (*"Fakku raqabah means emancipating a neck and liberating it from bondage; and after battle there is either unconditional grace [mann] or ransom [fidâ']"* — [CS-04, Vol. 22, pp. 155-160](https://shamela.ws/book/7798/14012)); Cessâs (*Ahkâmü'l-Kur'ân*, Vol. 5, pp. 178–183): «قَوْلُهُ تَعَالَى فَكَاتِبُوهُمْ إِنْ عَلِمْتُمْ فِيهِمْ خَيْرًا فِيهِ الْحَثُّ عَلَى الْمُكَاتَبَةِ وَإِعَانَتِهِمْ مِنْ مَالِ اللَّهِ» **[TRANSLATION]** (*"In God Almighty's statement 'Make a contract with them if you know there is within them goodness' lies emphatic encouragement for mukâtabah and assisting them from the wealth of God"* — [CS-09, Vol. 5, pp. 178-183](https://shamela.ws/book/23579/1724)); İbn Âşûr (*et-Tahrîr ve't-Tenvîr*, Vol. 26, pp. 78–86): «مَقَاصِدُ الشَّرِيعَةِ فِي الرِّقِّ تَنْتَهِي إِلَى إِبْطَالِهِ بِالتَّدْرِيجِ وَتَوْسِيعِ أَبْوَابِ الْحُرِّيَّةِ» **[TRANSLATION]** (*"The higher objectives [maqâsid] of the Shari'ah regarding bondage culminate in its gradual abolition and the widening of the doors of freedom"* — [CS-11, Vol. 26, pp. 78-86](https://shamela.ws/book/9776/8902)).[^4][^9][^11]
  - `[ANALYSIS]` `[YORUM]`: Classical exegesis demonstrates that `fakku raqabah` operates simultaneously as a cardinal moral virtue (Al-Balad 90:13), a contractual right (An-Nur 24:33), a statutory expiation (An-Nisa 4:92), and a public fiscal expenditure (At-Tawbah 9:60), establishing a multi-layered emancipatory architecture across Meccan and Medinan revelations.

### STEP 02 — LOGICAL SEMANTICS, PRIMARY ISNAD 2 EXEGESIS & COUNTER-PRINCIPLE TEST

- **Logical Condition-Consequence Structure:**
  - *Premise 1 (P1):* Human dignity is an inviolable divine endowment, and moral ascent (`al-'aqabah`) begins with `fakku raqabah` (Al-Balad 90:11–16).
  - *Premise 2 (P2):* Servitude is an accidental historical pathology whose causes must be dismantled (`ibtâlu sababi'r-riqq` — Râzî, [CS-07](https://shamela.ws/book/23635/5114)).
  - *Premise 3 (P3):* Entry into captivity is restricted strictly to active defensive warfare and terminated via unconditional release (`mann`) or ransom (`fidâ'`) (Muhammad 47:4).
  - *Premise 4 (P4):* Exit doors are multiplied through contractual self-redemption (`mukâtabah`, An-Nur 24:33), expiatory emancipation (`kaffârah`, An-Nisa 4:92), and public Zakat funds (`fi'r-riqâb`, At-Tawbah 9:60).
  - *Conclusion (C):* The Qur'an does not sanctify slavery; rather, it institutes a systemic cause-effect architecture for its gradual abolition.
- **Addressee & Language Safety:** Addressed to the Muslim community and legal institutions. Language safety requires preserving the exact modal nuances between imperative obligations (`fa-kâtibûhum`, `wa fi'r-riqâb`, `fa-tahrîru raqabah`) and ethical exhortations without lapsing into either apologetic denial or anachronistic distortion.
- **Counter-Principle Test:** Testing the objection *"Does the Qur'an legitimize slavery by regulating it?"*: (a) No verse ever characterizes slavery as a permanent normative ideal; (b) Muhammad 47:4 restricts battlefield captivity to `mann` (grace) or `fidâ'` (ransom) without commanding enslavement (`istirqâq`); (c) emancipation is elevated into worship, expiation, and state fiscal policy. `[ANALYSIS / YORUM]` The tension resolves through **gradual structural abolition** (*al-tadrîj fi'l-ibtâl*).

### STEP 03 — TRIADIC FRAMEWORK (ETHOS / LOGOS / PATHOS) & IS-OUGHT BRIDGE

- **Ethos (Moral Responsibility):** Grounding human dignity (*karâmah*) in universal creation (intersecting with Al-Isra 17:70), elevating the bondsperson to a moral brother/sister and legal subject (`An-Nisa 4:25, 36`).
- **Logos (Rational Coherence):** Linking the etymological contingency of `r-q-q` (weakness), contractual self-redemption (`mukâtabah` in An-Nur 24:33), and institutional Zakat financing (`wa fi'r-riqâb` in At-Tawbah 9:60) into a coherent cause-effect system (`[CITATION / NAKİL]` Râzî, *Mefâtîhu'l-Gayb*, Beirut: Dâru İhyâi't-Türâsi'l-Arabî, Vol. 28, pp. 38–44, [shamela.ws/book/23635/5114](https://shamela.ws/book/23635/5114)). `[CITATION / NAKİL]` In classical *furû' al-fiqh*, Serahsî (*el-Mebsût*, Vol. 8, p. 3, [shamela.ws/book/5423/1617](https://shamela.ws/book/5423/1617)) records scholarly consensus (*ijmâ'*) on the validity and normative recommendation of `mukâtabah`: «ثُمَّ جَوَازُ هَذَا الْعَقْدِ ثَبَتَ بِالنَّصِّ قَالَ اللَّهُ تَعَالَى: {وَاَلَّذِينَ يَبْتَغُونَ الْكِتَابَ مِمَّا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُمْ فَكَاتِبُوهُمْ إنْ عَلِمْتُمْ فِيهِمْ خَيْرًا} [النور: ٣٣] . وَبِظَاهِرِ الْآيَةِ يَقُولُ دَاوُد وَمَنْ تَابَعَهُ إذَا طَلَبَ الْعَبْدُ مِنْ مَوْلَاهُ أَنْ يُكَاتِبَهُ وَقَدْ عَلِمَ الْمَوْلَى فِيهِ خَيْرًا يَجِبُ عَلَيْهِ أَنْ يُكَاتِبَهُ لِأَنَّ الْأَمْرَ يُفِيدُ الْوُجُوبَ.» **[TRANSLATION]** (*"Furthermore, the permissibility of this [mukâtabah] contract is established by scriptural text, for God Almighty said: 'And those who seek a contract among those whom your right hands possess—then make a contract with them if you know there is within them goodness' [An-Nur 24:33]. Adhering to the apparent imperative of the verse, Dâwûd al-Zâhirî and his followers hold that when a bondsperson requests a mukâtabah contract from his master and the master knows there is goodness in him, it is obligatory upon the master to contract with him because the imperative denotes obligation [wujûb]"*), while establishing mandatory emancipation (`wujûb al-'itq`) in manslaughter expiation (An-Nisa 4:92): «اعْلَمْ بِأَنَّ الْإِعْتَاقَ لُغَةً هُوَ إحْدَاثُ الْقُوَّةِ يُقَالُ: عَتَقَ الْفَرْخُ إذَا قَوِيَ فَطَارَ عَنْ، وَكْرِهِ. وَفِي الشَّرِيعَةِ عِبَارَةٌ عَنْ إحْدَاثِ الْمَالِكِيَّةِ وَالِاسْتِبْدَادِ لِلْآدَمِيِّ، وَمِنْ ضَرُورَتِهِ، انْتِفَاءُ صِفَةِ الْمَمْلُوكِيَّةِ، وَالرِّقِّ» **[TRANSLATION]** (*"Know that i'tâq [emancipation] lexically means generating strength—as it is said 'ataqa al-farkhu' when a fledgling bird grows strong and flies from its nest. In the Shari'ah, it denotes establishing legal ownership capacity and independent agency for the human being, the necessary consequence of which is the negation of chattel status and bondage"* — [shamela.ws/book/5423/1434](https://shamela.ws/book/5423/1434), Vol. 7, p. 60).[^19][^20] `[CITATION / NAKİL]` Kâsânî (*Bedâi'u's-Sanâi'*, Vol. 4, p. 134, [shamela.ws/book/8183/1044](https://shamela.ws/book/8183/1044)) defines the legal essence of `mukâtabah` as «وَفِي الِاسْتِحْسَانِ جَائِزٌ بِالْكِتَابِ وَالسُّنَّةِ وَإِجْمَاعِ الْأُمَّةِ. أَمَّا الْكِتَابُ فَقَوْلُهُ ﷿ ﴿فَكَاتِبُوهُمْ إِنْ عَلِمْتُمْ فِيهِمْ خَيْرًا﴾ [النور: ٣٣] وَأَدْنَى دَرَجَاتِ الْأَمْرِ النَّدْبُ. فَكَانَتْ الْكِتَابَةُ مَنْدُوبًا إلَيْهَا فَضْلًا عَنْ الْجَوَازِ، وَقَوْلُهُ ﷿ ﴿إِنْ عَلِمْتُمْ فِيهِمْ خَيْرًا﴾ [النور: ٣٣] أَيْ رَغْبَةً فِي إقَامَةِ الْفَرَائِضِ، وَقِيلَ: وَفَاءً لِأَمَانَةِ الْكِتَابَةِ، وَقِيلَ: حِرْفَةً.» **[TRANSLATION]** (*"By juristic preference [istihsân], mukâtabah is valid by the Book, the Sunnah, and the consensus of the community. As for the Book, it is the statement of God Almighty: 'Make a contract with them if you know there is within them goodness' [An-Nur 24:33], and the minimum degree of the imperative is recommendation [nadb], making kitâbah normatively recommended beyond mere permissibility; and 'if you know there is within them goodness' means devotion to fulfilling religious duties, fidelity to the trust of the contract, or possession of an earning craft"*), proving that the contract immediately vests the bondsperson with independent legal and commercial agency (`yadan hâlan`).[^21]
- **Pathos (Affective & Moral Resonance):** Al-Balad 90:11–16 pairs `fakku raqabah` (breaking through the steep pass by freeing a neck) directly with feeding a near orphan on a day of severe famine (`yatîman dhâ maqrabah`) and a destitute person in the dust (`miskînan dhâ matrabah`), awakening deep moral conscience.
- **Hume's Guillotine (`Is-Ought` Epistemic Bridge):** `[ANALYSIS / YORUM]` The historical fact (*Is*) that war captivity and debt bondage were globally entrenched across 7th-century Roman, Byzantine, Sasanian, and Arabian economies cannot logically justify the normative claim (*Ought*) that slavery is perpetually legitimate—doing so commits a direct violation of **Hume's Guillotine**. In Qur'anic legal philosophy, the normative baseline (*Ought*) is primordial human freedom (`al-aslu fi'l-insâni al-hurriyyah`). The Qur'an bridges empirical reality (*Is*) toward the normative ideal (*Ought*) by narrowing the entry gate to zero peacetime enslavement (Muhammad 47:4) while institutionalizing four high-capacity exit gates (Al-Balad 90:13, An-Nur 24:33, An-Nisa 4:92, At-Tawbah 9:60).

### STEP 04 — MAQASID AL-SHARIAH ANALYSIS (DARURIYYAT / HAJIYYAT / TAHSINIYYAT)

- **Hifz al-Nafs & Hifz al-'Ird (Daruriyyat — Preservation of Life, Personhood & Liberty):** Bodily and legal inviolability remains incomplete without liberty (`al-hurriyyah`). ### STEP 05 — INTRA-SURA CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS (SIYAQ-SIBAQ S1–S7)

- **S1 (Immediate Lexical Context):** In Muhammad 47:4, immediately after battlefield engagement (`fa-darba'r-riqâb`) and securing prisoners (`fa-shuddû'l-wathâq`), the verse specifies only two post-war outcomes: **`fa-immâ mannan ba'du wa immâ fidâ'an`** (either unconditional grace or ransom)—enslavement (`istirqâq`) is never textually commanded.
- **S2 (Intra-Passage Context):** An-Nur 24:32–33 unites marriage facilitation (`wa ankihû`), contractual emancipation (`fa-kâtibûhum`), financial grant (`wa âtûhum min mâlillâh`), and the strict prohibition of coercing female servants into prostitution (`wa lâ tukrihû fatayâtikum 'ala'l-bighâ'`) into a single charter of liberty and chastity.
- **S3 (Intra-Surah Structural Locus):** Surah Al-Balad (90:1–20) diagnoses human toil (`laqad khalaqna'l-insâna fî kabad`), critiques ostentatious wealth, and defines the supreme moral ascent (`al-'aqabah`) first and foremost as **`fakku raqabah`** (90:13).
- **S4 (Meccan-Medinan Gradualism):** In the Meccan period (Al-Balad 90:11–16), `fakku raqabah` was established as the ontological and moral pinnacle of piety; in the Medinan period (An-Nisa 4:92, An-Nur 24:33, At-Tawbah 9:60, Muhammad 47:4), it was institutionalized into binding expiation, enforceable contract law, state Zakat budgeting, and law of armed conflict.
- **S5 (Historical Context / *Asbab al-Nuzul*):** An-Nur 24:33 was revealed when 'Abdullâh b. Ubayy b. Salûl attempted to coerce his female servants Mu'âdhah and Musaykah into prostitution for financial gain, and when Subayh, the bondsperson of Huwaytib b. 'Abdil-'Uzzâ, requested a `mukâtabah` contract—proving direct scriptural intervention in defense of liberty and bodily integrity (`[CITATION / NAKİL]` Taberî, *Câmi'u'l-Beyân*, Beirut: Müessesetü'r-Risâle, Vol. 22, pp. 155–160, [shamela.ws/book/7798/14012](https://shamela.ws/book/7798/14012)).[^4]
- **S6 (Canonical Mushaf Coherence):** Al-Isra 17:70 (`wa laqad karramnâ Banî Âdam`) and Al-Hujurat 49:13 (`inna akramakum 'indallâhi atqâkum`) establish the supreme constitutional framework (universal human dignity and *taqwâ*-based equality) governing all verses on servitude.
- **S7 (Historical-Legal Context & Context-Stripping Audit):** Whereas Roman, Byzantine, and Sasanian law classified the slave as "a speaking tool" (*instrumentum vocale*) devoid of legal personality, the Qur'an recognized bondspeople as legal subjects capable of binding contracts (`mukâtabah`), marriage, and public state funding (`fi'r-riqâb`), dismantling the institution from within. `[CITATION / NAKİL]` İbn Kudâme (*el-Muğnî*, Vol. 14, pp. 344 & 442) affirms that emancipation is among the highest acts of devotion: «العِتْقُ فى اللُّغَةِ: الخُلوصُ. ومنه عِتاقُ الْخَيْلِ، وعِتاقُ الطِّيْرِ، أى خالِصَتُها، وسُمِّىَ البَيْتُ الحَرامُ عَتِيقًا، لخُلوصِهِ من أَيدى الجبابِرَةِ. وهو فى الشَّرْعِ: تَحْريرُ الرَّقَبَةِ، وتخليصُها من الرِّق. يُقال (١) : عَتَقَ العَبْدُ، وأعتَقْتُه أنا، وهو عَتِيقٌ، ومُعتَقٌ (٢) . والأَصْلُ فيه الكِتابُ، والسُّنَّةُ، والإِجْماعُ. أمَّا الكِتابُ، فقولُ اللَّه تعالى: {فَتَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ} (٣) . وقال اللَّه تعالى: {فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ} (٤) .» **[TRANSLATION]** (*"Al-'itq [emancipation] lexically means purity and deliverance; hence thoroughbred horses and noble birds are called 'itâq, and the Sacred House [Ka'bah] was named 'Atîq because of its deliverance from the hands of tyrants. - **Core Aporia:** `[ANALYSIS / YORUM]` The deepest methodological aporia of this topic lies in the tension between the Qur'an's strategy of directing the entrenched institution of bondage (`al-riqq`) toward **gradual structural abolition** without issuing an instantaneous single-day economic prohibition, and the historical reality that medieval imperial economies **froze** this abolitionist trajectory into a prolonged status quo. This monograph resolves the aporia not by concealing it, but by applying the *Usul al-Fiqh* distinction between *'illah* and *hikmah*: the normative cause (*'illah* — consolidating primordial human liberty, `al-hurriyyah al-asliyyah`) is permanent and binding, while the institutional instruments (`K1–K3`) realize its completion in history.
