### STEP 00-5 — LINGUISTIC MATRIX & SEMANTIC ANALYSIS (MANDATORY H6)

- **4-Dimensional Linguistic Matrix & Semantic Analysis (Mandatory H6 Protocol):**
  1. **`ٱلْعَدْلِ وَٱلْإِحْسَـٰنِ` (*Al-'Adli wa'l-Ihsan* — Roots `'a-d-l` & `h-s-n`):** As Al-Raghib al-Isfahani (*al-Mufradat*, p. 552) and Fakhr al-Din al-Razi (*Mafatih*, 20/98) explain with classic precision: **`al-'adl` (justice)** is giving exactly what you owe and taking only what is owed to you (symmetrical legal equity); **`al-ihsan` (moral excellence/beneficence)** is giving *more* than what is legally required of you and taking *less* than what is owed to you (asymmetrical grace, compassion, and beauty). *'Adl* is the foundation of law; *ihsan* is the pinnacle of ethics.
  2. **`وَإِيتَآئِ ذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ` (*Wa-ita'i dhi'l-qurba* — Primary Solidarity Network):** Active giving (*ita'*) to relatives and close community bonds from one's own rightful resources, preventing social atomization.
  3. **`ٱلْفَحْشَآءِ وَٱلْمُنكَرِ` (*Al-Fahsha'i wa'l-Munkar*):** Analyzed by Ibn Faris (*Maqayis*, 4/478) and Ibn Manzur (*Lisan al-Arab*, 6/324): `al-fahsha'` denotes gross personal/moral indecency driven by disordered appetite (*quwwah shahwaniyyah*), while `al-munkar` denotes any evil rejected by sound human intellect and revelation.
  4. **`وَٱلْبَغْىِ` (*Wa'l-Baghy* — Root `b-gh-y`):** Transgression, arrogance, tyranny, and abuse of power over others (*quwwah ghadabiyyah*).

### STEP 02 — LOGICAL SEMANTICS, PRIMARY ISNAD 2 EXEGESIS & COUNTER-PRINCIPLE TEST

- **Logical Condition-Consequence Structure:**
  - *Three Foundational Commands (`Inna'llaha ya'muru bi'l-'adli wa'l-ihsani wa-ita'i dhi'l-qurba`):* (1) Symmetrical legal justice (`'adl`), (2) Asymmetrical moral excellence & compassion (`ihsan`), and (3) Family/community solidarity (`ita'i dhi'l-qurba`).
  - *Three Foundational Prohibitions (`wa-yanha 'ani'l-fahsha'i wa'l-munkari wa'l-baghy`):* (1) Gross indecency (`fahsha'`), (2) Publicly recognized evil (`munkar`), and (3) Tyranny/oppression (`baghy`).

Caring for relatives is fulfilled from one's *personal* wealth and affection, whereas public trusts belong strictly to the qualified (`An-Nisa 4:58`, `Al-An'am 6:152`).

### STEP 13 — ENFÜSÎ-ÂFÂKÎ DIALECTIC & SPIRITUAL ETHICS (KALP FIKHI / İRFÂNÎ-İŞÂRÎ LAYER)

#### 13.1. Enfüsî-Âfâkî Dialectic (Ontological & Perceptual Level)
In the external constitutional, legal, and sociological order (*âfâk*), Surah al-Nahl 16:90 promulgates Islam's **comprehensive ethical constitution**, uniting three foundational positive mandates (`al-'Adl` — impartial legal and distributive justice, `al-Ihsân` — restorative benevolence and institutional excellence, and `Îtâ'i dhi'l-qurbâ` — kinship and communal solidarity) with three categorical prohibitions (`al-Fahshâ'` — moral indecency, `al-Munkar` — rationally and legally reprehensible wrong, and `al-Baghy` — tyrannical transgression and abuse of power). Yet according to the ontological mirror principle of Surah Fussilat 41:53 (*"We shall show them Our signs upon the horizons [âfâk] and within their own souls [enfüs]"*), outward bureaucratic callousness, social atomization, and institutional tyranny (`baghy`) are the macrocosmic projection of an inward imbalance within the human conscience (*enfüs / kalp fıkhı*): spiritual aridity devoid of `ihsân` and the corruption of the soul's three faculties—when the appetitive faculty transgresses, it breeds `fahshâ'`; when the rational faculty is perverted, it breeds `munkar`; and when the irascible/egoistic faculty swells with pride, it breeds `baghy` (lording over creation). Only through the inward **dialectic of Ihsan and Baghy**—extinguishing egoistic arrogance (`baghy`) through the living awareness of God's presence (`ihsân`)—can a society sustain both impartial justice (`'adl`) and compassionate solidarity in the outer world (*âfâk*).

#### 13.2. Jurisprudence of the Heart (*Kalp Fıkhı*) and Inner Pathologies (*Emrâzü'l-Kulûb*)
In classical *kalp fıkhı* (*fiqh al-qulûb*) literature, Surah al-Nahl 16:90—celebrated by Ibn Mas'ûd as the most comprehensive verse in the Qur'an (al-Tabarî, *Jâmi' al-Bayân*, 14/162)—is expounded as the ultimate interior scale balancing the virtues and vices of the heart:
