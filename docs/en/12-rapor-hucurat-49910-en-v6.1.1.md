### STEP 00-5 — LINGUISTIC MATRIX & SEMANTIC ANALYSIS (MANDATORY H6)

- **4-Dimensional Linguistic Matrix & Semantic Analysis (Mandatory H6 Protocol):**
  1. **`فَأَصْلِحُوا۟` (*Fa-aslihu* — Root `s-l-h`):** Derived from *salah* (the direct opposite of *fasad* / corruption and ruin; Ibn Faris, *Maqayis*, 3/303). Appearing three times across verses 9–10, it signifies active, structural peacemaking that removes the root causes of conflict rather than a superficial truce.
  2. **`بَغَتْ` (*Baghat* — Root `b-gh-y`):** Defined by Ibn Manzur (*Lisan al-Arab*, 14/75) as transgressing just bounds (`mujawazat al-hadd`), arrogance, and wrongful aggression. In Islamic public law (*Fiqh Ahl al-Baghy*), a group becomes *baghi* when it rejects peaceful arbitration and uses armed force to impose its will.
  3. **`حَتَّىٰ تَفِىٓءَ إِلَىٰٓ أَمْرِ ٱللَّهِ` (*Hatta tafi'a ila amri'llah* — Root `f-y-'`):** From *fay'* (the return of shade after the sun passes the zenith), meaning to return to the rule of law and legitimate arbitration. As Al-Jassas (*Ahkam*, 5/280) and Al-Qurtubi (*al-Jami'*, 16/317) establish, the limit particle `hatta` strictly bounds collective force: its sole aim is compelling the aggressor to return to the negotiating table—never total annihilation.
  4. **`بِٱلْعَدْلِ وَأَقْسِطُوٓا۟` (*Bi'l-'adli wa-aqsitu* — Root `q-s-t`):** Form IV *if'al* (`aqsata`), where the *hamzah* is *li'l-izalah* (removal): literally 'remove all injustice and restore equity!' Even after stopping an aggressor (`baghi`), the peace settlement must be free of victor's vengeance.

- **Canonical English Translation (Sahih International):**
  > *"And if two factions among the believers should fight, then make peace (fa-aṣliḥū) between the two. But if one of them oppresses and aggresses against (baghat) the other, then fight against the one that aggresses until it returns (tafī’a) to the ordinance of Allah. And if it returns, then make peace between them with justice (bi'l-‘adl) and act equitably (wa aqsiṭū); indeed, Allah loves those who act equitably. (9) The believers are but brothers, so make reconciliation between your brothers and fear Allah that you may receive mercy. (10)"*

- **Word-by-Word Lexical & Morphological Breakdown (*Al-Tahlil al-Lafzi*):**
  - `وَإِن طَآئِفَتَانِ مِنَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ ٱقْتَتَلُوا۟` (Wa in ṭā’ifatāni mina'l-mu’minīna iqtatalū): And if two groups of believers fall into armed conflict.
  - `فَأَصْلِحُوا۟ بَيْنَهُمَا` (fa-aṣliḥū baynahumā): Immediately mediate and make peace between them (Stage 1: preventive diplomacy and mediation).
  - `فَإِنۢ بَغَتْ إِحْدَىٰهُمَا عَلَى ٱلْأُخْرَىٰ` (fa-in baghat iḥdāhumā ‘ala'l-ukhrā): But if one of them commits aggression / rejects arbitration (`baghy`).
  - `فَقَـٰتِلُوا۟ ٱلَّتِى تَبْغِى` (fa-qātilu'llatī tabghī): Then collectively restrain and fight the aggressor faction (Stage 2: collective security enforcement).
  - `حَتَّىٰ تَفِىٓءَ إِلَىٰٓ أَمْرِ ٱللَّهِ` (ḥattā tafī’a ilā amri'llāh): Until it returns to the rule of law and divine justice (limiting force strictly to restoring legality, not annihilation).
  - `فَإِن فَآءَتْ فَأَصْلِحُوا۟ بَيْنَهُمَا بِٱلْعَدْلِ وَأَقْسِطُوٓا۟` (fa-in fā’at fa-aṣliḥū baynahumā bi'l-‘adli wa aqsiṭū): And once it returns, reconcile them with strict justice and equity (Stage 3: non-punitive post-conflict justice).
  - `إِنَّمَا ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ إِخْوَةٌ` (innama'l-mu’minūna ikhwatun): The believers are but brothers (preserving moral fraternity even during conflict resolution).

- **Classical Exegetical Synthesis on Lexical Order (Al-Qurtubi & Fakhr al-Din al-Razi):**

  - `[ANALYSIS]`: Al-Hujurat 49:9–10 synthesizes mediation (`iṣlāḥ`), collective deterrence against aggression (`baghy`), and restorative post-conflict equity (`qisṭ`).

### STEP 02 — LOGICAL SEMANTICS, PRIMARY ISNAD 2 EXEGESIS & COUNTER-PRINCIPLE TEST

- **Logical Condition-Consequence Structure:**
  - *Stage 1 (`Wa-in ta'ifatani mina'l-mu'minina iqtatalu fa-aslihu baynahuma`):* Outbreak of conflict between two factions → Immediate impartial mediation (`islah`).
  - *Stage 2 (`Fa-in baghat ihdahuma 'ala'l-ukhra fa-qatilu'llati tabghi hatta tafi'a ila amri'llah`):* Refusal of mediation and continued aggression (`baghy`) by one faction → Collective, strictly proportional deterrence until the aggressor returns to the rule of law (`hatta tafi'a`).
  - *Stage 3 (`Fa-in fa'at fa-aslihu baynahuma bi'l-'adli wa-aqsitu`):* Cessation of aggression → Just, non-vindictive settlement (`bi'l-'adli wa'l-qist`) grounded in fraternal solidarity (`Innama'l-mu'minuna ikhwatun`, `49:10`).
- **Counter-Principle Test:** Treating internal political or armed conflict as reason for excommunication (*takfir*) or total war of annihilation violates the explicit **Counter-Principles** of `49:9–10`: the Quran continues to call both fighting factions `mina'l-mu'minin` (from the believers) and `ikhwatun` (brothers), and strictly limits intervention to `hatta tafi'a` (until it returns to legal order).

#### 13.1. Enfüsî-Âfâkî Dialectic (Ontological & Perceptual Level)
In the external sphere of public law and international conflict resolution (*âfâk*), Surah al-Hujurât 49:9–10 establishes a three-stage collective security and peacebuilding architecture to end factional warfare, polarization, and armed aggression (`baghy`) through impartial mediation (`fa-aslihû`), bounded deterrence (`hattâ tafî'a ilâ amri'llâh`), and non-vindictive restorative equity (`bi'l-'adli wa-aqsitû`), anchored in **fraternal reconciliation** (`innama'l-mu'minûna ikhwatun`). Yet according to the ontological mirror principle of Surah Fussilat 41:53 (*"We shall show them Our signs upon the horizons [âfâk] and within their own souls [enfüs]"*), outward communal warfare and political transgression (`baghy`) are the macrocosmic projection of an inward pathology within the human conscience (*enfüs / kalp fıkhı*): blind tribal or sectarian partisanship (*hamiyyat al-jâhiliyyah* / *'asabiyyah*), envy (*hasad*), vindictive rage (*tashaffî*), and the **insurrection of the nafs** (*baghy al-nafs*) against the spiritual heart. Decision-makers who have not subdued the rebellious tyranny of their own ego in the inner world (*enfüs*) cannot act as impartial peacemakers (*muslihûn*) or equitable judges (*muqsitûn*) in the outer world (*âfâk*); durable external peace is sustainable only when grounded in inward justice (`qist`) and reverent God-consciousness (`wa-ttaqû'llâha la'allakum turhamûn`).
