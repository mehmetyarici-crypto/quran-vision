### STEP 00-5 — LINGUISTIC MATRIX & SEMANTIC ANALYSIS (MANDATORY H6)

- **4-Dimensional Linguistic Matrix & Semantic Analysis (Mandatory H6 Protocol):**
  1. **`ٱلْفَىْءُ` (*Al-Fay'* — Root `f-y-'`):** Literally 'the returning shade' (Ibn Faris, *Maqayis*, 4/466). In Islamic fiscal law, *fay'* denotes public revenue and wealth acquired peacefully without cavalry or camel combat (`ma afa'a'llahu 'ala Rasulihi`). Etymologically, calling public wealth *fay'* signifies that wealth is like temporary afternoon shade that must return to benefit the entire society rather than being hoarded permanently.
  2. **`دُولَةًۢ` (*Dulatan* — Root `d-w-l`):** Analyzed by Ibn Manzur (*Lisan al-Arab*, 11/252) and Al-Zamakhshari (*al-Kashshaf*, 4/499): with a *dammah* (`dūlah`), it denotes tangible wealth or financial capital that circulates exclusively hand-to-hand within a closed circle; with a *fathah* (`dawlah`), it denotes political turn or power.
  3. **`كَىْ لَا يَكُونَ دُولَةًۢ بَيْنَ ٱلْأَغْنِيَآءِ مِنكُمْ` (*Kay la yakuna dulatan bayna'l-aghniya'i minkum* — Explicit Economic Teleology):** Uses the explicit particle of purpose (`kay la` = *so that it does not become*), establishing a universal macroeconomic *'illah* and *maqsid*: wealth and capital must **never** become a closed oligarchic circuit among the rich alone.
  4. **`وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ ۖ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ شَدِيدُ ٱلْعِقَابِ` (*Inna'llaha shadidu'l-'iqab*):** Sealing a fiscal redistribution verse with 'severe in punishment' warns that oligarchic hoarding of public wealth is a grave systemic transgression.

- **Canonical English Translation (Sahih International):**
  > *"And what Allah restored (afā’a) to His Messenger from the people of the towns — it is for Allah and for the Messenger and for [his] near relatives and the orphans and the needy and the [stranded] traveler — so that it will not be a perpetual monopoly circulating solely among the rich from among you (kay lā yakūna dūlatan bayna'l-aghniyā’i minkum). And whatever the Messenger has given you — take; and what he has forbidden you — refrain from. And fear Allah; indeed, Allah is severe in penalty. (7)"*

- **Word-by-Word Lexical & Morphological Breakdown (*Al-Tahlil al-Lafzi*):**
  - `مَّآ أَفَآءَ ٱللَّهُ` (Mā afā’a'llāhu): Whatever public revenue Allah has returned (*fay’* literally means 'the returning of a shadow', signifying that wealth ultimately belongs to public welfare).
  - `فَلِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ` (fa-li'llāhi wa li'r-rasūli): Belongs to Allah and the Messenger (i.e., the public treasury and foundational civic infrastructure).
  - `وَلِذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ وَٱلْيَتَـٰمَىٰ وَٱلْمَسَـٰكِينِ وَٱبْنِ ٱلسَّبِيلِ` (wa li-dhi'l-qurbā wa'l-yatāmā wa'l-masākīni wa-bni's-sabīl): And to near kin, orphans, the poor, and stranded travelers/refugees (targeted fiscal transfers to vulnerable groups).
  - `كَىْ لَا يَكُونَ دُولَةًۢ` (kay lā yakūna dūlatan): In order that wealth does not become a closed-circuit monopoly (`dūlah` denotes a commodity passed exclusively within a privileged circle).
  - `بَيْنَ ٱلْأَغْنِيَآءِ مِنكُمْ` (bayna'l-aghniyā’i minkum): Exclusively among the wealthy elite among you!
  - `وَمَآ ءَاتَىٰكُمُ ٱلرَّسُولُ فَخُذُوهُ وَمَا نَهَىٰكُمْ عَنْهُ فَٱنتَهُوا۟` (wa mā ātākumu'r-rasūlu fa-khudhūhu wa mā nahākum ‘anhu fa-ntahū): And whatever the Messenger gives/commands you, take it; and whatever he forbids you, abstain from it.

- **Classical Exegetical Synthesis on Lexical Order (Ibn 'Ashur & Al-Jassas):**

  - `[ANALYSIS]`: While safeguarding private property, Al-Hashr 59:7 mandates anti-monopoly regulation and redistributive fiscal policy so that economic growth circulates across all social strata.

### STEP 13 — ENFÜSÎ-ÂFÂKÎ DIALECTIC & SPIRITUAL ETHICS (KALP FIKHI / İRFÂNÎ-İŞÂRÎ LAYER)

#### 13.1. Enfüsî-Âfâkî Dialectic (Ontological & Perceptual Level)
In the external macroeconomic, fiscal, and constitutional order (*âfâk*), Surah al-Hashr 59:7 establishes the foundational principle of **circulatory wealth ethics**: preventing public resources and economic surplus from becoming a closed-circuit monopoly circulating solely among a wealthy oligarchy (`kay lâ yakûna dûlatan bayna'l-aghniyâ'i minkum`) and channeling wealth toward orphans, the destitute, and the vulnerable. Yet according to the ontological mirror principle of Surah Fussilat 41:53 (*"We shall show them Our signs upon the horizons [âfâk] and within their own souls [enfüs]"*), external oligarchic concentration (`dûlah`) and predatory rent-seeking are the macrocosmic projection of an inward moral disease within the human conscience (*enfüs / kalp fıkhı*): possessive greed (*shuhh al-nafs*), the **pathology of acquisitiveness** (*takâthur*), hoarding (*kanz*), and the tyrannical illusion of self-sufficiency (*istighnâ'*). It is no coincidence that immediately after legislating macro-distributive justice in al-Hashr 59:7, the Qur'an diagnoses its inward spiritual root in al-Hashr 59:9: *"And whoever is protected from the covetousness of his own soul (`wa-man yûqa shuhha nafsihî`)—it is those who are the truly successful"*, praising the ethic of altruistic preference (*îthâr*). Without curing the inward **pathology of acquisitiveness** (`shuhh`) in the heart (*enfüs*), external fiscal systems (*âfâk*) are inevitably captured by plutocratic interests.

#### 13.2. Jurisprudence of the Heart (*Kalp Fıkhı*) and Inner Pathologies (*Emrâzü'l-Kulûb*)
In classical *kalp fıkhı* (*fiqh al-qulûb*) literature, oligarchic wealth concentration (`dûlah`), monopolistic hoarding (*ihtikâr*), and indifference to poverty are traced to the inward maladies of love of wealth (*hubb al-mâl*), miserliness (*bukhl*), and possessive greed (*shuhh*):
- **Al-Hârith al-Muhâsibî (d. 243/857), *al-Ri'âyah li-Huqûqillâh*:** In his analysis of worldly covetousness and delusion (*ghurûr*), al-Muhâsibî demonstrates that when the ego (*nafs*) regards wealth as personal sovereignty rather than a divine trust (*amânah*), it deadens empathy for the poor and breeds tyrannical arrogance, a pathology cured only through ascetic detachment of the heart (*zuhd*) and conscientious spending in God's path (*infâq*) (al-Muhâsibî, *al-Ri'âyah li-Huqûqillâh*, ed. 'Abd al-Qâdir Ahmad 'Atâ, Beirut: Dâr al-Kutub al-'Ilmiyyah, pp. 295–325; Digital access: [shamela.ws/book/6875/108](https://shamela.ws/book/6875/108)).
- **Abû Hâmid al-Ghazâlî (d. 505/1111), *Ihyâ' 'Ulûm al-Dîn*:** In *Kitâb Dhamm al-Bukhl wa Dhamm Hubb al-Mâl* (Condemnation of Miserliness and Love of Wealth, Vol. 3), al-Ghazâlî explains that currency and wealth were created to circulate through society like blood in a living body or an impartial magistrate bridging human needs; those who lock wealth in closed circuits (`dûlah` and `kanz`) commit treason against the divine wisdom of circulation and enslave their own hearts to metal. Al-Ghazâlî identifies the supreme remedy for `shuhh al-nafs` as the station of *îthâr* (altruistic preference, al-Hashr 59:9), the pinnacle of generosity (*sakhâ'*) (al-Ghazâlî, *Ihyâ' 'Ulûm al-Dîn*, Beirut: Dâr al-Ma'rifah, Vol. 3, pp. 231–273; Digital access: [shamela.ws/book/9472/977](https://shamela.ws/book/9472/977)).
- **Enfüs–Policy Bridge:** Because policymakers and financial elites who remain captive to inward covetousness (`shuhh`) in their conscience (*enfüs*) design regressive tax codes and rentier bailouts in the public sphere (*âfâk*), the institutional decision branches in `STEP 15` (`K1` sovereign public-resource redistribution, `K2` anti-monopoly and asset-backed real-economy finance, and `K3` institutional *zakâh* and *waqf* circulation) establish binding structural checks against oligarchic hoarding.

     - **Response 2 (Responding Group):** Not at all. Immediately following `59:7`, verses `59:8–9` praise the Ansâr for generously sharing their legitimately owned homes and properties (`îthâr`). Without rightful ownership, neither *zakâh*, *infâq*, *waqf*, nor *îthâr* could exist. What `59:7` prohibits is not productive ownership, but oligarchic monopoly (`dûlah`) and the exclusion of orphans, the poor, and wayfarers from economic circulation.
