### STEP 13 — ENFÜSÎ-ÂFÂKÎ DIALECTIC & SPIRITUAL ETHICS (KALP FIKHI / İRFÂNÎ-İŞÂRÎ LAYER)

#### 13.1. Enfüsî-Âfâkî Dialectic (Ontological & Perceptual Level)
In the external constitutional, legal, and socioeconomic order (*âfâk*), Surah al-An'âm 6:151–152 promulgates a universal moral charter (`al-Muhkamât`) that protects child life from economic despair (`min imlâq`), safeguards orphan property (`mâla'l-yatîm`), mandates **probity in commerce** and metrological fairness (`awfû'l-kayla wa'l-mîzâna bi'l-qist`), and enforces judicial impartiality even against one's own kin (`wa-law kâna dhâ qurbâ`). Yet in light of the ontological mirror principle of Surah Fussilat 41:53 (*"We shall show them Our signs upon the horizons [âfâk] and within their own souls [enfüs]"*), outward fiduciary betrayal, commercial fraud (*tatfîf*), and nepotistic corruption are the macrocosmic projection of an inward pathology within the human conscience (*enfüs / kalp fıkhı*): caprice (*hawâ*), existential anxiety over sustenance (*khawf al-faqr* / distrust in God the Provider), and heedlessness toward **esoteric transgressions** (*al-fawâhish al-bâtinah*). By commanding `wa-lâ taqrabû'l-fawâhisha mâ zahara minhâ wa-mâ batan` (*"And do not approach indecencies—what is outward of them and what is inward/hidden"*, 6:151), the Qur'an establishes an indivisible moral law: one cannot maintain an honest scale in the marketplace (`mâ zahara`) while harboring hidden greed, duplicity, and partisan bias in the heart (`mâ batan`).

#### 13.2. Jurisprudence of the Heart (*Kalp Fıkhı*) and Inner Pathologies (*Emrâzü'l-Kulûb*)
In classical *kalp fıkhı* (*fiqh al-qulûb*) literature, the violation of fiduciary trusts, commercial shortchanging, and biased testimony are traced to inward maladies of scrupulousness (*qillat al-wara'*), covetousness (*hirs*), and weak trust in Divine Providence (*da'f al-tawakkul*):
- **Al-Hârith al-Muhâsibî (d. 243/857), *al-Ri'âyah li-Huqûqillâh*:** In *Bâb al-Wara' wa Tark al-Shubuhât* (Scrupulousness and Abandoning Doubtful Matters), al-Muhâsibî expounds `mâ zahara minhâ wa-mâ batan` (6:151) by demonstrating that abstaining from outward unlawful acts of the limbs is incomplete without purifying the heart from **esoteric transgressions**—such as hidden greed for orphan wealth, ostentation (*riyâ'*), and subtle partiality toward relatives—anchoring all public justice in inward scrupulousness (*wara'*) (al-Muhâsibî, *al-Ri'âyah li-Huqûqillâh*, ed. 'Abd al-Qâdir Ahmad 'Atâ, Beirut: Dâr al-Kutub al-'Ilmiyyah, pp. 65–90).
- **Abû Hâmid al-Ghazâlî (d. 505/1111), *Ihyâ' 'Ulûm al-Dîn*:**
  - `[CITATION / NAKİL]`: In *Kitâb Âdâb al-Kasb wa'l-Ma'âsh* (Ethics of Earning and Livelihood, Vol. 2, pp. 61–87) and *Kitâb al-Tawakkul* (Vol. 4, p. 245, [shamela.ws/book/9472/1404](https://shamela.ws/book/9472/1404)), al-Ghazâlî anchors commercial honesty and trust in Divine provision in the following verbatim statement:

  - `[TRANSLATION]`: *"Ibrâhîm al-Khawwâs recited the Almighty's words, 'And place your trust in the Ever-Living who never dies,' to the end of the verse, and then said: 'After this verse, it does not befit a servant to seek refuge or reliance in anyone other than Allah the Exalted.'"*
  - `[ANALYSIS / YORUM]`: Through this principle, al-Ghazâlî explains that the divine assurance `nahnu narzuqukum wa-iyyâhum` (6:151) uproots the inward panic of poverty (*khawf al-faqr*) that drives people to ruthlessness, while the command `awfû'l-kayla wa'l-mîzâna bi'l-qist` (6:152) requires **probity in commerce** not merely as mechanical accuracy on the scales, but as inward sincerity (*nasîhah*) that refuses to conceal defects or exploit a buyer's ignorance.

##### C. Stage 3: Critical Inquiry to Prevent Religious Exploitation (Minutes 17–28) — Opposite Panel (*Zıt Panel*) Case Scenario
- **Instructional Tool:** **Opposite Panel** (`DAB4.4, DKAB.12.3.5, DKAB.11.4.3, E3.4`).
- **Case Scenario:** *"A merchant publicly displays religious slogans in his shop and prides himself on outward piety, yet secretly shortchanges customers through deceptive packaging, underpays an orphaned apprentice working for him, and gives biased testimony in court to protect a wealthy cousin. When confronted, he claims: 'Business requires cleverness, and helping family comes first; God will forgive minor commercial tricks because I pray.' Students interrogate this claim using Surah al-An'âm 6:151–152."*
- **Panel Focus Questions & Analytical Responses:**
  1. **Question 1 (Questioning Group):** *"How does Surah al-An'âm 6:151–152—the 'Universal Ten Commandments' of the Qur'an—refute the separation of ritual piety from orphan rights, honest weights, and judicial impartiality (`DKAB.12.3.5`, `DKAB.11.4.3`)?"*
     - **Response 1 (Responding Group):** In `Al-An'âm 6:151–152`, God binds the prohibition of polytheism (`allâ tushrikû bihî shay'an`) directly to protecting orphan property, giving full measure and weight with equity (`awfû al-kayla wa'l-mîzâna bi'l-qist`), and speaking justly even against close kin (`wa-law kâna dhâ qurbâ`) within a single sealed covenant (`dhâlikum wassâkum bih`). Using religious symbols to mask commercial fraud (*tatfîf*) and nepotism violates the *muhkam* foundation of Islam.
  2. **Question 2 (Questioning Group):** *"Why does the prohibition `mâ zahara minhâ wa-mâ batan` (what is apparent of indecencies and what is concealed) apply directly to hidden commercial fraud and digital deception?"*
     - **Response 2 (Responding Group):** Classical exegetes (Ibn Manzûr, al-Qurtubî) explain that `mâ batan` encompasses both covert sins committed behind closed doors (or hidden inside deceptive contracts/algorithms) and inward spiritual diseases such as hypocrisy (*riyâ'*) and greed. Moral integrity admits no split between public appearance and hidden practice.
