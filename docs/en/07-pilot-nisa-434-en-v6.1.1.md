### STEP 00-5 — LINGUISTIC MATRIX & SEMANTIC ANALYSIS (MANDATORY H6)

- **4-Dimensional Linguistic Matrix & Semantic Analysis (Mandatory H6 Protocol):**
  1. **`قَوَّٰمُونَ` (*Qawwamuna* — Root `q-w-m`):** Intensive active participle (*fa''al*) denoting one who stands up to shoulder a burden, provide service, and protect—not a tyrannical ruler. As Al-Zamakhshari (*al-Kashshaf*, 1/505) and Al-Jassas (*Ahkam*, 3/148) establish, *qiwamah* is a functional fiduciary responsibility tied strictly to two conditions: innate capacities (`bima faddala'llahu ba'dahum 'ala ba'd`) and financial maintenance (`wa-bima anfaqu min amwalihim`).
  2. **`نُشُوزَهُنَّ` (*Nushuzahunna* — Root `n-sh-z`):** Defined by Ibn Faris (*Maqayis*, 5/430) as a high, protruding patch of ground (`nashz`), signifying marital rupture, hostility, and rebellion against the family covenant. Importantly, the Quran also uses the exact same term `nushuz` for husbands who mistreat or abandon their wives in **An-Nisa 4:128** (`wa-in imra'atun khafat min ba'liha nushuzan aw i'radan`), proving that *nushuz* is a bilateral concept of marital breach.
  3. **`وَٱضْرِبُوهُنَّ` (*Wa-dribuhunna* — Root `d-r-b`):** Analyzed by Ibn Manzur (*Lisan al-Arab*, 1/544) as a polysemous root ranging from physical striking to separating/departing (`daraba fi'l-ard`) and setting an example. Classical jurists (Ibn 'Abbas, 'Ata' ibn Abi Rabah, Al-Shafi'i, Al-Qurtubi) restricted it via the Prophetic Sunnah to a purely symbolic, non-injurious gesture (*ghayr mubarrih*, such as with a *siwak*) or temporary separation, while declaring its abandonment (*tark*) superior.
  4. **`فَلَا تَبْغُوا۟ عَلَيْهِنَّ سَبِيلًا` (*Fa-la tabghu 'alayhinna sabilan* — Prohibition of *Baghy*):** 'Do not seek any way of oppression/injustice against them; indeed, Allah is Most High, Grand (`'Aliyyan Kabira`).' By invoking His own supreme majesty at the end of the verse, God warns husbands that any abuse of domestic power is an act of *baghy* (tyranny) accountable before the Most High.
  5. **Qur'anic Semantic Conceptualization (Râgıb el-İsfahânî):** `[CITATION]` Râgıb el-İsfahânî (*el-Müfredât fî Garîbi'l-Kur'ân*, ed. Safvân Adnân Dâvûdî, Dımaşk: Dârü'l-Kalem, 1412/1992, p. 691, [shamela.ws/book/23636/673](https://shamela.ws/book/23636/673)):[^10] «جَعَلَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ قِياماً [النساء/ ٥] ، أي: جعلها ممّا يمسككم. وقوله: جَعَلَ اللَّهُ الْكَعْبَةَ الْبَيْتَ الْحَرامَ قِياماً لِلنَّاسِ [المائدة/ ٩٧] أي: قِوَاما لهم يقوم به معاشهم ومعادهم» — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"Regarding the verse 'God has made [your property] a means of support (qiyāman) for you' [An-Nisā' 4:5], it means He made it that by which you are sustained and upheld; and His statement 'God has made the Ka'bah, the Sacred House, a support (qiyāman) for humanity' [Al-Mā'idah 5:97] means a sustaining pillar (qiwām) by which their worldly livelihood and hereafter stand."* `[ANALYSIS]` Râgıb el-İsfahânî's definition of *qiwâm* as the sustaining support by which an order stands grounds *qawwâmûn* semantically in protective stewardship and financial responsibility rather than domination.

### STEP 01 — LEXICAL ANALYSIS, INTRA-SURA COHERENCE & HISTORICAL CONTEXT [V12]

[CITATION] **Surah An-Nisa (4:34)** constitutes one of the foundational normative pillars of Quranic governance, ethics, and civilizational order.
[ANALYSIS] Within the 26-step **Quran Vision (v6.3.0)** analytical architecture, this report examines **Intra-Family Governance, Fiduciary Responsibility (Qawwamah) & Graduated De-Escalation in Marital Crisis** by uniting verified classical Islamic scholarship (**ISNAD 2nd Edition**, 5 primary Arabic sources) with falsifiable contemporary empirical indicators (**APA 7 / DOI**, 3 global institutional datasets).

- **Primary Quranic Text (Surah An-Nisa 4:34 — Resm-i Uthmani):**

- **Canonical English Translation (Sahih International):**
  > *"Men are caretakers and protectors (qawwāmūna) of women, by [right of] what Allah has given one over the other in complementary capacities and what they spend [for maintenance] from their wealth. So righteous women are devoutly obedient, guarding in [the husband's] absence what Allah would have them guard. But those from whom you fear destructive marital discord (nushūz) — [first] advise them; [then if they persist], forsake them in bed; and [finally], set forth a symbolic boundary. But if they return to harmony, seek no means of oppression against them. Indeed, Allah is ever Exalted and Grand. (34)"*

- **Word-by-Word Lexical & Morphological Breakdown (*Al-Tahlil al-Lafzi*):**
  - `ٱلرِّجَالُ قَوَّٰمُونَ عَلَى ٱلنِّسَآءِ` (Ar-rijālu qawwāmūna ‘ala'n-nisā’): Men are fiduciary protectors, maintainers, and responsible stewards of the family institution (`qawwām` denotes moral and financial service, not despotism).
  - `بِمَا فَضَّلَ ٱللَّهُ بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍۢ` (bimā faḍḍala'llāhu ba‘ḍahum ‘alā ba‘ḍ): By virtue of the complementary endowments Allah has distributed among them (`ba‘ḍahum ‘alā ba‘ḍ` indicates reciprocal complementarity).
  - `وَبِمَآ أَنفَقُوا۟ مِنْ أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ` (wa bimā anfaqū min amwālihim): And because they spend from their wealth (making *qawwāmah* a conditional legal-financial duty tied to *mahr* and maintenance).
  - `حَـٰفِظَـٰتٌۭ لِّلْغَيْبِ بِمَا حَفِظَ ٱللَّهُ` (ḥāfiẓātun li'l-ghaybi bimā ḥafiẓa'llāh): Guarding family trust and privacy in the spouse's absence by virtue of Allah's protection of their rights.
  - `نُشُوزَهُنَّ` (nushūzahunna): Their severe marital breach / hostility threatening the family covenant (just as male *nushūz* is condemned in An-Nisa 4:128).
  - `فَعِظُوهُنَّ` (fa-‘iẓūhunna): Stage 1 — Counsel them with wisdom, gentleness, and constructive dialogue.
  - `وَٱهْجُرُوهُنَّ فِى ٱلْمَضَاجِعِ` (wa-hjurūhunna fi'l-maḍāji‘): Stage 2 — Separate in the sleeping quarters (signaling emotional gravity inside the home without verbal abuse or expulsion).
  - `وَٱضْرِبُوهُنَّ` (wa-ḍribūhunna): Stage 3 — Restricted strictly by the Prophetic Sunnah (*ghayr mubarriḥ* — non-harmful symbolic gesture or temporary separation, as the Prophet ﷺ never struck any woman).

  - `إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيًّۭا كَبِيرًۭا` (inna'llāha kāna ‘aliyyan kabīrā): Indeed, Allah is Most High, Most Great (a direct divine warning reminding husbands that Allah stands above them to defend the wronged).

- **Classical Exegetical Synthesis on Lexical Order (Al-Tabari, Al-Razi, Al-Qurtubi, Al-Zamakhshari & Ibn 'Ashur):**
  - `[CITATION]`: Al-Zamakhshari (*al-Kashshaf*, Vol. 2, p. 71) emphasizes that the prohibition `fa-lā tabghū ‘alayhinna sabīlā` paired with the divine attributes `‘Aliyyan Kabīrā` legally forbids domestic tyranny and links 4:34 directly to the bilateral family arbitration court (*ḥakamayn*) in 4:35. **Taberî** (*Câmi'u'l-Beyân 'an Te'vîli Âyi'l-Kur'ân*, Vol. 6, p. 690, [shamela.ws/book/7798/4528](https://shamela.ws/book/7798/4528)) grounds the financial condition of *qiwâmah* as follows: «وأما قوله: ﴿وَبِمَا أَنْفَقُوا مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ﴾. فإنه يعنى: وبما ساقُوا إليهن من صَداقٍ، وأنفَقُوا عليهن من نَفَقةٍ»[^11] — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"As for His statement 'and because they spend from their wealth', it means: on account of the dower (mahr) they have given them and the maintenance (nafaqah) they have expended upon them."* **Fahreddîn er-Râzî** (*Mefâtîhu'l-Gayb (et-Tefsîru'l-Kebîr)*, Vol. 10, p. 71, [shamela.ws/book/23635/1739](https://shamela.ws/book/23635/1739)) explains the lexical order of rights in the verse: «قَانِتَاتٌ، أَيْ مُطِيعَاتٌ للَّه، حافِظاتٌ لِلْغَيْبِ أَيْ قَائِمَاتٌ بِحُقُوقِ الزَّوْجِ، وَقَدَّمَ قَضَاءَ حَقِّ اللَّه ثُمَّ أَتْبَعَ ذَلِكَ بِقَضَاءِ حَقِّ الزَّوْجِ»[^12] — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"'Qānitāt' means obedient to God, and 'ḥāfiẓātun li'l-ghayb' means upholding the rights of the husband in his absence; thus God placed the fulfillment of His own right first and followed it with the fulfillment of the spouse's right."* **Kurtubî** (*el-Câmi' li-Ahkâmi'l-Kur'ân*, Vol. 5, p. 168, [shamela.ws/book/20855/1838](https://shamela.ws/book/20855/1838)) defines the syntactic and legal scope of *qawwâmûn* as: «الرِّجالُ قَوَّامُونَ عَلَى النِّساءِ ابْتِدَاءٌ وَخَبَرٌ، أَيْ يَقُومُونَ بِالنَّفَقَةِ عَلَيْهِنَّ وَالذَّبِّ عَنْهُنَّ»[^13] — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"'Men are qawwāmūn over women' is a subject-predicate construction meaning: they undertake the duty of providing financial maintenance for them and defending/protecting them."* Likewise, **İbn Âşûr** (*et-Tahrîr ve't-Tenvîr*, Vol. 5, p. 38, [shamela.ws/book/9776/1894](https://shamela.ws/book/9776/1894)) establishes its morphological meaning: «وَالْقَوَّامُ: الَّذِي يَقُومُ عَلَى شَأْنِ شَيْءٍ وَيَلِيهِ وَيُصْلِحُهُ، يُقَالُ: قَوَّامٌ وقيّام وقيّوم وقيّم»[^14] — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"'Al-qawwām' is the one who attends to the affairs of something, takes charge of its care, and sets it right; one says qawwām, qiyyām, qayyūm, and qayyim."*
  - `[ANALYSIS]`: Read together with 4:35 and the Prophetic Sunnah, Taberî, Fahreddîn er-Râzî, Kurtubî, Zemahşerî, and İbn Âşûr demonstrate that *qiwâmah* is not ontological superiority, but a functional responsibility anchored in dower, financial maintenance (*bimâ anfaqû*), and protection, while 4:34–35 institutes a graduated, non-violent de-escalation protocol aimed at saving the family from impulsive divorce and domestic abuse.

#### 13.2. Jurisprudence of the Heart & Inward Pathologies (*Fiqh al-Batin* — Methodology of al-Ghazali & al-Muhasibi)
- **[CITATION]** While outward jurisprudence (*fiqh al-zahir*) defines the contractual and financial obligations of marriage, the jurisprudence of the heart (*fiqh al-batin* / *kalp fıkhı*) diagnoses and remedies the hidden spiritual ailments that corrode marital harmony. **Hâris el-Muhâsibî** (d. 243/857), in *er-Ri'âye li-Hukûkillâh* (ed. 'Abd al-Qadir Ahmad 'Ata, Beirut: Dar al-Kutub al-'Ilmiyyah, p. 140, [shamela.ws/book/6875/129](https://shamela.ws/book/6875/129)), defines the spiritual disease of arrogance (*kibr*) that fuels domestic tyranny: «الْكبر أَن يتعظم على غَيره أَنَفَة مِنْهُ واحتقارا لَهُ»[^16] — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"Arrogance (al-kibr) is to exalt oneself above another out of disdain and contempt for them."* **Abu Hamid al-Ghazali** (d. 505/1111), in *Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din* under *Kitab Adab al-Nikah* (Beirut: Dar al-Ma'rifah, Vol. 2, p. 42, [shamela.ws/book/9472/402](https://shamela.ws/book/9472/402)), sets forth the ethical duties of the husband: «أما الزوج فعليه مراعاة الاعتدال والأدب فِي اثْنَيْ عَشَرَ أَمْرًا فِي الْوَلِيمَةِ وَالْمُعَاشَرَةِ»[^17] — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"As for the husband, it is incumbent upon him to observe moderation, fairness, and refined etiquette across twelve matters concerning the marriage banquet and marital companionship (al-mu'āsharah)."* Furthermore, in *Kitab Dhamm al-Kibr wa-al-'Ujb* (Vol. 3, p. 343, [shamela.ws/book/9472/1089](https://shamela.ws/book/9472/1089)), al-Ghazali cites the criterion of true humility: «وقال أبو يزيد ما دام العبد يظن أن في الخلق من هو شر منه فهو متكبر فقيل له فمتى يكون متواضعاً قال إذا لم ير لنفسه مقاماً ولا حالاً»[^18] — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"Abū Yazīd al-Bisṭāmī said: 'So long as a servant imagines that there is anyone among creation worse than himself, he is arrogant.' When asked, 'Then when does he become truly humble?' he replied: 'When he claims neither superior station nor spiritual state for himself.'"*
- **[ANALYSIS]** Al-Ghazali and al-Muhasibi establish that true moral nobility in family life (*husn al-khuluq*) does not merely consist in refraining from harming one's spouse, but in patiently enduring difficulties, restraining wrath, and uprooting the ego's desire for domination (*kibr*). This spiritual standard is sealed by the Prophetic maxims: *"The strong person is not the one who overpowers others by wrestling, but the one who controls himself when angry"* (al-Bukhari, *Adab*, 76, Hadith No: 6114; Muslim, *Birr*, 107, Hadith No: 2609) and the Farewell Sermon declaration: *"Fear God regarding women, for you have taken them as a sacred trust from God [bi-amanatillah]"* (Muslim, *Hajj*, 147, Hadith No: 1218).

#### 13.3. Classical Spiritual / *Ishari* Exegesis (al-Qushayri — *Lata'if al-Isharat* & Sehl et-Tüsterî)
- **[CITATION]** **'Abd al-Karim al-Qushayri** (d. 465/1072) articulates the inward spiritual signification of *qiwamah* and *nushuz* in An-Nisa 4:34 in his *Lata'if al-Isharat* as follows:
  > «خَصَّ الرِّجَالَ بِالْقُوَّةِ فَزِيدَ بِالْحَمْلِ عَلَيْهِمْ؛ فَالْحَمْلُ عَلَى حَسَبِ الْقُوَّةِ. وَالْعِبْرَةُ بِالْقُلُوبِ وَالْهِمَمِ لَا بِالنُّفُوسِ وَالْجُثَثِ... أَيِ ارْتَقُوا فِي تَهْذِيبِهِنَّ بِالتَّدْرِيجِ وَالرِّفْقِ، وَإِنْ صَلُحَ الْأَمْرُ بِالْوَعْظِ فَلَا تَسْتَعْمِلِ الْعَصَا بِالضَّرْبِ، فَالْآيَةُ تَتَضَمَّنُ آدَابَ الْعِشْرَةِ»
  > **[TRANSLATION]:** *"God distinguished men with strength, and therefore increased the burden of responsibility placed upon them; for the burden is proportional to capacity. Yet true worth lies in spiritual hearts and noble aspirations, not in lower egos and physical bodies... Meaning: ascend in refining and counseling them gradually and with gentle kindness (bi'l-tadrīj wa'l-rifq); and if the matter is set right through admonition, do not resort to striking, for the verse encompasses the refined ethics of marital companionship (ādāb al-'ishrah)."*
  > — **ISNAD 2 Citation:** 'Abd al-Karim b. Hawazin al-Qushayri, *Lata'if al-Isharat*, ed. Ibrahim Basyuni (Cairo: al-Hay'ah al-Misriyyah al-'Ammah li-al-Kitab, 1981), Vol. 1, p. 330 (cf. Ahmad b. Muhammad Ibn 'Ajibah, *al-Bahr al-Madid fi Tafsir al-Qur'an al-Majid*, Cairo, 1999, Vol. 1, pp. 502–504). **Digital Library Link:** [shamela.ws/book/23629/327](https://shamela.ws/book/23629/327) / [Altafsir.com Classical Spiritual Exegesis Database](https://www.altafsir.com/Tafasir.asp?tMadhNo=3&tTafsirNo=31&tSoraNo=4&tAyahNo=34&tDisplay=yes).[^9]
  Likewise, **Sehl et-Tüsterî** (d. 283/896), in *Tefsîru't-Tüsterî* (p. 53, [shamela.ws/book/23600/50](https://shamela.ws/book/23600/50)), highlights the spiritual grace underlying the marital gift (*mahr*): «سئل عن قوله: ﴿وَآتُوا النِّساءَ صَدُقاتِهِنَّ نِحْلَةً﴾ [٤] قال: أعطوهن الصداق هبة من الله ﷿ لهن»[^19] — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"When asked about God's statement 'And give women their marital gifts (ṣaduqātihinna) graciously (niḥlatan)' [An-Nisā' 4:4], he said: 'Give them their dower as a pure gift bestowed upon them from God the Exalted.'"*
- **[ANALYSIS]** **Exegetical & Hermeneutic Analysis:** While preserving the outward ethical-legal protections of family law, Imam al-Qushayri, Sehl et-Tüsterî, and Ibn 'Ajibah transpose *qiwamah* from an instrument of interpersonal domination into the **inward vigilance of the intellect and spiritual heart over the rebellious lower ego (*al-nafs al-ammarah*)** and emphasize that marital rights rest upon divine gift,gradual gentleness (*rifq*), and refined companionship (*ādāb al-'ishrah*). One whose intellect and heart fail to exercise *qiwamah* over his own lower passions cannot cultivate a home of tranquility (*sakan*) and mercy (Ar-Rum 30:21).

### Z-2 · DIALECTICAL DISPUTE MATRIX (SCHOOLS OF JURISPRUDENCE & EXEGESIS) [MANDATORY V3/V8]

[ANALYSIS] Classical and contemporary scholars have articulated distinct legal positions on the conditional nature of *qiwamah* and the restriction of the third stage in An-Nisa 4:34. In compliance with **Mandatory Rule V3/V8**, these positions are mapped below:

| Exegetical / Legal School | Key Authorities & Sources | Position on *Qiwamah* & the Restriction of `Wa-dribuhunna` | Primary Evidentiary Basis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prophetic Sunnah Restriction & *Tark* Priority (*Classical Majority*)** | Ibn 'Abbas, Al-Shafi'i (*al-Umm*), Al-Tabari, Al-Jassas (*Ahkam*, 3/150), Al-Qurtubi | *Qiwamah* is conditional upon maintenance (`nafaqah`). The third stage is strictly limited by Prophetic *takhsis* to a symbolic, painless gesture (*ghayr mubarrih*, e.g., with a *siwak* or folded cloth) that leaves no mark, and abandoning it (*tark*) is explicitly preferred. | Farewell Sermon (*ghayr mubarrih*; Muslim, *Ḥajj*, 147, no. 1218), Muslim (*Faḍā'il*, 79, no. 2328 — *'The Prophet never struck a woman'*), and Abū Dāwūd (*Nikāḥ*, 42, no. 2146 — *'Those who do so are not the best of you'*). |
| **Categorical Prohibition of Harm & State Ban (*Athari / Maqasid School*)** | 'Ata' ibn Abi Rabah (d. 114 AH), Ibn al-'Arabi (*Ahkam al-Qur'an*), Ibn Ashur (*al-Tahrir*, 5/43) | 'Ata' ruled that a husband may not strike his wife even with a *siwak*, expressing frustration only through non-violent displeasure (`ghadab`). - **Methodological Synthesis:** This monograph unites the **Classical Sunnah Restriction (*Ghayr Mubarrih / Tark*)** with **Ibn Ashur's Maqasid State Prohibition** (`al-Tahrir`, 5/43): in modern legal administration, any physical or psychological battery constitutes forbidden *baghy* and *darar*, and marital crises must be channeled directly into counseling and `4:35` bilateral arbitration.
