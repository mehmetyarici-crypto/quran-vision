### STEP 00-5 — LINGUISTIC MATRIX & SEMANTIC ANALYSIS (MANDATORY H6)

- **4-Dimensional Linguistic Matrix & Semantic Analysis (Mandatory H6 Protocol):**
  1. **`ٱلسَّارِقُ وَٱلسَّارِقَةُ` (*Al-Sariqu wa'l-Sariqatu* — Root `s-r-q`):** As Ibn Faris (*Maqayis*, 3/157) and Al-Zamakhshari (*al-Kashshaf*, 2/235) explain, *sariqah* in Arabic lexicography does not mean any taking of property; it denotes taking protected property stealthily from a secure custody (`hirz`) without any share of ownership or necessity. Furthermore, the noun form (*ism al-fa'il*: `al-sariq`) denotes deliberate, uncoerced agency.
  2. **`فَٱقْطَعُوٓا۟ أَيْدِيَهُمَا` (*Fa-qta'u aydiyahuma* — Root `q-t-'`):** Analyzed by Ibn Manzur (*Lisan al-Arab*, 8/276) as cutting or severing/stopping. In classical *fiqh* (Al-Jassas, *Ahkam*, 4/62), this maximum *hadd* penalty cannot be applied unless over a dozen stringent legal preconditions are simultaneously satisfied: minimum statutory value (`nisab`), secure custody (`hirz`), absence of famine or necessity (`idtirar`), absence of public treasury share (`shubhat al-milk`), and zero evidentiary doubt.
  3. **`نَكَـٰلًۭا مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ` (*Nakalan mina'llah* — Deterrence):** *Nakal* literally means an exemplary deterrent restraint (related to `nikl`, the iron bit on a bridle that restrains a horse from bolting). Its purpose is systemic crime prevention rather than frequent physical mutilation.
  4. **`عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ` (*'Azizun Hakim* — Pairing Power with Wisdom):** Classical authorities retrieve the profound anecdote where a Bedouin heard a reader recite `5:38` ending with *Ghafurun Rahim* and objected: *'That cannot be the word of Allah; if He forgave and had mercy here, He would not have commanded cutting. Rather, He was Mighty (`'Aziz`) and Wise (`Hakim`), so He legislated deterrence; then in the very next verse (`5:39`) He said: But whoever repents after his wrongdoing and reforms (`wa-aslaha`), Allah turns to him in forgiveness—indeed Allah is Forgiving, Merciful!'*
  5. **Qur'anic Semantic Conceptualization (Râgıb el-İsfahânî):** `[CITATION]` Râgıb el-İsfahânî (*el-Müfredât fî Garîbi'l-Kur'ân*, ed. Safvân Adnân Dâvûdî, Damascus: Dârü'l-Kalem, 1412/1992, p. 408, [shamela.ws/book/23636/390](https://shamela.ws/book/23636/390)):[^13] «السَّرِقَةُ: أخذ ما ليس له أخذه في خفاء، وصار ذلك في الشّرع لتناول الشيء من موضع مخصوص، وقدر مخصوص» — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"Sariqah (theft) is taking in secret what one has no right to take; in Sacred Law (al-shar') it has come to denote taking an item from a specifically custodied place (mawḍi' makhṣūṣ / ḥirz) in a specific minimum quantity (qadr makhṣūṣ / niṣāb)."* `[ANALYSIS]` This semantic boundary excludes petty taking from unguarded places or acts driven by acute famine.

  - `وَٱلسَّارِقُ وَٱلسَّارِقَةُ` (Wa's-sāriqu wa's-sāriqatu): The habitual/willful male and female thief (active participle *ism al-fā‘il* denoting deliberate clandestine theft of protected property above the *niṣāb* threshold, excluding famine or necessity).

  - `جَزَآءًۢ بِمَا كَسَبَا` (jazā’an bimā kasabā): As a just recompense for what they freely earned (*kasb* requires full volition; coercion, hunger, or systemic deprivation removes *kasb* and suspends the penalty).
  - `نَكَـٰلًۭا مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ` (nakālan mina'llāh): As an exemplary deterrent from Allah to safeguard public property (*ḥifẓ al-māl*).

- **Classical Exegetical Synthesis on Lexical Order (Al-Tabari, Al-Razi, Al-Zamakhshari & Al-Qurtubi):**
  - `[CITATION]`: Al-Zamakhshari (*al-Kashshaf*, Vol. 2, p. 230) explains that `bimā kasabā` restricts the statutory penalty to willful enrichment where the state has already secured basic subsistence—the exact legal ground upon which Caliph 'Umar ibn al-Khattab suspended the penalty during the Year of Famine (*‘Ām al-Ramādah*). **Taberî** (*Câmi'u'l-Beyân*, Vol. 8, p. 407, [shamela.ws/book/7798/5694](https://shamela.ws/book/7798/5694)) expounds the conditional syntax of the verse: «يقولُ جلَّ ثناؤُه: ومَن سرَق مِن رجلٍ أو امرأةٍ فاقْطَعُوا أيُّها الناسُ يدَه. ولذلك رُفِع وَالسَّارِقُ وَالسَّارِقَةُ؛ لأنهما غيرُ مُوقَّتين»[^14] — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"God the Exalted says: Whoever steals, whether man or woman, O people (public judicial authority), cut off their hand; and for that reason 'wa'l-sāriqu wa'l-sāriqatu' are placed in the nominative case, because they are not restricted to a specific individual but carry universal conditional force."* **Fahreddîn er-Râzî** (*Mefâtîhu'l-Gayb*, Vol. 11, p. 351, [shamela.ws/book/23635/2016](https://shamela.ws/book/23635/2016)) analyzes this grammatical structure: «اخْتَلَفَ النَّحْوِيُّونَ فِي الرَّفْعِ فِي قَوْلِهِ وَالسَّارِقُ وَالسَّارِقَةُ عَلَى وُجُوهٍ: الْأَوَّلُ: وَهُوَ قَوْلُ سِيبَوَيْهِ وَالْأَخْفَشِ: أَنَّ قَوْلَهُ وَالسَّارِقُ وَالسَّارِقَةُ مَرْفُوعَانِ بِالِابْتِدَاءِ»[^15] — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"The grammarians differed regarding the nominative case in His statement 'wa'l-sāriqu wa'l-sāriqatu' across several views; the first, held by Sībawayh and al-Akhfash, is that 'wa'l-sāriqu wa'l-sāriqatu' are in the nominative as subjects of an inchoative conditional clause (bi'l-ibtidā')."* **Kurtubî** (*el-Câmi' li-Ahkâmi'l-Kur'ân*, Vol. 6, p. 159, [shamela.ws/book/20855/2256](https://shamela.ws/book/20855/2256)) links the verse to the preceding passage on armed brigandage: «لَمَّا ذَكَرَ تَعَالَى أَخْذَ الْأَمْوَالِ بِطَرِيقِ السَّعْيِ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَالْفَسَادِ ذَكَرَ حُكْمَ السَّارِقِ مِنْ غير حراب على ما يأتي»[^16] — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"After God the Exalted mentioned the seizure of property by way of spreading corruption in the land and armed assault (ḥirābah), He mentioned here the ruling of the thief (al-sāriq) who steals covertly without armed warfare, as follows."*

### STEP 02 — LOGICAL SEMANTICS, PRIMARY ISNAD 2 EXEGESIS & COUNTER-PRINCIPLE TEST

- **Logical Condition-Consequence Structure:**
  - *Strict Definitional Condition (`Al-Sariqu wa'l-Sariqatu`):* Clandestine theft of property above the `nisab` threshold from secure custody (`hirz`) by a sane adult acting without famine, poverty-driven necessity, or ownership doubt.
  - *Maximum Deterrent Ruling (`fa-qta'u aydiyahuma ... nakalan`):* Maximum deterrent sanction, immediately suspended (*isqat*) if any legal or social doubt (`shubhah`) exists (Prophetic Hadith: *Idra'u al-hududa bi'l-shubuhat*).
  - *Rehabilitative Sequel (`5:39 — fa-man taba min ba'di zulmihi wa-aslaha`):* Repentance, restitution, and moral/social rehabilitation.
- **Counter-Principle Test:** Enforcing harsh criminal penalties against the poor during economic crisis or famine violates the foundational **Counter-Principles** of Islamic law: the Prophetic maxim *'Avert fixed penalties in the presence of doubts (`shubuhat`)'* (al-Tirmidhī, *Ḥudūd*, 2, no. 1424; Ibn Mājah, *Ḥudūd*, 5, no. 2545), Caliph 'Umar ibn al-Khattab's suspension of the theft *hadd* during the Year of Famine (*'Am al-Ramadah*, 18 AH; Mālik, *al-Muwaṭṭa'*, *Aqḍiyah*, 26; 'Abd al-Razzāq, *al-Muṣannaf*, 10/242, no. 18990), and the Quranic mandate of social security (`Al-Ma'arij 70:24–25` — *'In their wealth is a known right for the beggar and the deprived'*).

### STEP 03 — TRIADIC FRAMEWORK (ETHOS / LOGOS / PATHOS) & IS-OUGHT BRIDGE

- **Ethos (Moral Responsibility):** Protecting honest labor and property security (`Hifz al-Mal`) while requiring the state to guarantee every citizen's subsistence needs before enforcing severe penal sanctions.
- **Logos (Rational Coherence):** As Cesare Beccaria and Islamic *Maqasid* jurists (Al-Shatibi, Ibn Ashur) concur, deterrence depends not on indiscriminate brutality, but on social justice, certainty of fair adjudication, and rehabilitative reintegration (`5:39`). Where the state fails to provide basic livelihood, poverty becomes a legal `shubhah` (doubt) that suspends maximum punishment.
- **Pathos (Affective & Moral Resonance):** Pairing `5:38` (`'Azizun Hakim` — majestic deterrence against predatory theft) with `5:39` (`Ghafurun Rahim` — divine forgiveness for whoever repents and reforms) balances public security with redemptive compassion.
- **Hume's Guillotine (`Is-Ought` Epistemic Bridge):** Empirical crime statistics and Gini inequality metrics (*Is*) do not alter the moral prohibition of theft (*Ought*); however, under Islamic legal theory (*tahqiq al-manat*), empirical poverty and subsistence deficits directly establish the presence of `shubhah` (evidentiary/social doubt), shifting the applicable legal ruling from `hadd` to social welfare and restorative `ta'zir`.

### STEP 04 — MAQASID AL-SHARIAH ANALYSIS (DARURIYYAT / HAJIYYAT / TAHSINIYYAT)

- **Hifz al-Mal (Preservation of Property & Honest Labor):** Safeguards private and public property from predatory theft, embezzlement, and systemic corruption.
- **Hifz al-Nafs & Hifz al-'Ird (Preservation of Life & Human Dignity):** Protects both the needy (via the `shubhah` and famine exemption) and the repentant offender (via `5:39` rehabilitation) from unjust destruction.
- **Hifz al-Din & Public Trust:** Prevents the grotesque distortion whereby petty survival theft is harshly punished while elite public embezzlement (*ghulul*) goes unpunished (Hadith of Usamah ibn Zayd in al-Bukhārī, *Ḥudūd*, 11–12, no. 6788; Muslim, *Ḥudūd*, 8–9, no. 1688).
- **Shatibi, Al-Sarakhsi & Ibn Ashur Maqasid Hierarchy:** `[CITATION]` In classical Hanafi jurisprudence, **Serahsî** (*el-Mebsût*, *Kitâbü's-Sirka*, Vol. 9, p. 133, [shamela.ws/book/5423/1934](https://shamela.ws/book/5423/1934)) defines the strict legal and linguistic essence of theft (*sariqah*) around secure custody (*hirz*): «السَّرِقَةُ لُغَةً أَخْذُ مَالِ الْغَيْرِ عَلَى وَجْهِ الْخُفْيَةِ سُمِّيَ بِهِ؛ لِأَنَّهُ يُسَارِقُ عَيْنَ حَافِظِهِ وَيَطْلُبُ غُرَّتَهُ لِيَأْخُذَهُ»[^17] — **[TRANSLATION]:** *"Sariqah in language is taking the property of another by way of secrecy; it was named thus because the thief steals away from the eye of its custodian (ḥāfiẓ) and watches for a moment of heedlessness to take it."* `[ANALYSIS]` At the **Daruriyyat** level, `5:38–39` secures property rights and subsistence justice; at the **Hajiyyat** level, Serahsî's *Kitâbü's-Sirka* and the doctrine of *dar' al-hudud bi'l-shubuhat* prevent judicial error wherever custody (*hirz*) or subsistence security is compromised; at the **Tahsiniyyat** level, `5:39` reintegrates the reformed individual into society without lifelong stigma.
- **Contemporary Islamic Legal Historiography & the Doubt Canon (*Dar' al-Hudud bi'l-Shubuhat*):** `[ANALYSIS]` Contemporary legal scholarship confirms that the Qur'anic imperative `فَاقْطَعُوا أَيْدِيَهُمَا` (*"cut off their hands"*) in Al-Ma'idah 5:38 was never operationalized as mechanical literalism, but was systematically bounded by rigorous procedural, evidentiary, and socio-economic safeguards. Ali Bardakoğlu, in the TDV *Encyclopedia of Islam* entry "Hırsızlık" (Vol. 17, pp. 384–396), documents that classical jurists across the schools required over twenty cumulative conditions—including custody in a secure locus (*hirz*), attainment of the minimum value threshold (*nisab*), absence of any ownership ambiguity (*shubhat al-milk*), and absence of famine or necessity (*darurah*)—for the *hadd* penalty to apply.[^9] Intisar A. Rabb, in *Doubt in Islamic Law* (2015) and *Islamic Legal Maxims as Substantive Canons of Construction* (2010, pp. 63–125), demonstrates that the prophetic and juristic doubt canon (`ادرؤوا الحدود بالشبهات` — *"Avoid hudud punishments in cases of doubt"*) functioned as a foundational substantive canon of construction whereby jurists actively converted corporal punishment into discretionary or restitutive remedies whenever factual, legal, or socio-economic doubt arose.[^10][^11] Similarly, Rudolph Peters (*Crime and Punishment in Islamic Law*, 2005, pp. 55–57) establishes through historical court records that strict *nisab*, *hirz*, and evidentiary thresholds made actual judicial amputation exceedingly rare in pre-modern Islamic courts, while preserving the verse's powerful moral deterrence against predatory property crime.[^12]

#### 13.1. Inward-Outward (*Anfus–Afaq*) Dialectic (Ontological & Perceptual Plane)
- **[ANALYSIS]** In the Qur'anic order of socioeconomic and penal justice, the external architecture of property security, public trust integrity, and social welfare (*afaq*) and the inward moral consciousness of contentment (*qana'ah*), trustworthiness (*amanah*), and lawful livelihood (*halal*) (*anfus*) form an indivisible whole. The legal equilibrium established by **Al-Ma'idah 5:38–39** against predatory theft (*sariqah*), white-collar public embezzlement (*ghulul*), and poverty-driven desperation in the outer social sphere (*afaq*) is directly mirrored in the inward struggle against covetousness (*tama'*), insatiable greed (*shuhh*), worldly grasping (*tul al-amal*), and indifference to the rights of others (*anfus*). In accordance with **Fussilat 41:53**, rulers and societies that fail to sever the inward hands of greed and avarice within their own hearts cannot combat corruption and systemic plunder in the public sphere. Equally, the restorative portal opened immediately in **Al-Ma'idah 5:39** (*"But whoever repents after his wrongdoing and reforms, indeed God turns to him in forgiveness"*) demonstrates that outward penal rehabilitation (`K3` in **Step 15**) achieves completion only through inward remorse (*nadamah*), restitution of rights, and moral reformation (*islah al-qalb* / *enfüs / kalp fıkhı*).
